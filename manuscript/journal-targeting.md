# Journal targeting memo

## Recommended first target: *Phytochemistry Reviews*

This is the strongest scope match for the current manuscript because the central contribution is a critical synthesis of *Artemisia* specialized chemistry: mono-, sesqui-, diterpene, triterpene, and sesquiterpene-lactone classes, linked to biosynthesis, analytical context, chemotype variation, and biological evidence. The comparative-genomics and antiparasitic sections should be presented as the manuscript's differentiating framework rather than as claims of a completed pharmacological meta-analysis.

Journal page: https://link.springer.com/journal/11101

Submission guidelines: https://link.springer.com/journal/11101/submission-guidelines (checked 2026-09-04)

The current Springer guidance identifies this as a review-article venue, asks review authors to provide the search strategy, keywords, and (where applicable) exclusion criteria, requires a title page with authors, affiliations, corresponding-author e-mail, and ORCID iDs where available, limits the abstract to 150–250 words and keywords to 4–6, and requires a Statements and Declarations section. The journal specifies Word submission, no more than three levels of displayed headings, author–year citations, an alphabetized reference list with full DOI links where available, consecutive Arabic numbering for tables, and descriptive figure captions. It also requests a cover letter explaining scope fit, listing three to five relevant original research articles by the authors, and stating whether the submission is unsolicited or associated with a special issue. The package supplies these structures and a Word artifact; author-specific fields remain open until the submitting authors confirm them. The journal’s current research-data policy encourages repository deposition and data citation; this review proactively provides a direct Terpedia KB location and pinned source provenance for all generated evidence tables, screening outputs, comparative-genomics diagnostics, scripts, figures, and checksums.

### Positioning for submission

Use a title and abstract that foreground chemical evolution and evidence quality, for example:

> *Artemisia* terpene diversity across scales: chemotype, biosynthetic evolution, and bounded antiparasitic evidence

The cover letter should emphasize four contributions:

1. an auditable, specimen-aware synthesis across terpene classes;
2. explicit separation of chemical identification, pathway evidence, and parasite phenotype;
3. a comparative evolutionary-genomics framework that identifies testable gene–metabolite hypotheses; and
4. a reusable Terpedia evidence package with source identifiers, extraction tables, screening provenance, and checksums.

The submission is now framed as a frozen, source-auditable scoping review rather than a living or completed systematic review. Source-level verification is complete for the 114 queued sources and 133 linked evidence rows; the quantitative-eligibility audit documents a conservative descriptive-only synthesis with zero pooling candidates. The broad multi-species/common-reannotation orthology analysis remains unfinished, although the frozen two-proteome OrthoFinder result is complete and archived. Terpedia may be updated, but later records must be versioned as outside this frozen analysis unless the manuscript is formally updated. Figures are original package artwork and are supplied in editable/vector and raster forms; any future third-party material must be cleared separately before submission.

## Ambitious alternative: *Journal of Experimental Botany*

JXB is attractive if the manuscript is rewritten around a broad plant-biology question—how specialized-metabolite pathway evolution, tissue compartmentalization, and environmental regulation generate chemical diversity in a non-model lineage. Its current author guidance says reviews should synthesize intensive current research and stimulate new ideas, and unsolicited reviews require a presubmission enquiry. It also warns that reviews that merely catalogue the literature are unlikely to be considered.

Journal guidance: https://academic.oup.com/jxb/pages/what-does-jxb-publish

This target therefore requires a stronger conceptual model, a completed or clearly bounded comparative analysis, and a presubmission enquiry with the proposed title, abstract, scope paragraph, key recent references, and author list.

## Practical alternatives

*Journal of Ethnopharmacology* is appropriate if the manuscript is recentered on traditional use, pharmacological evidence, parasite assays, toxicology, and translational boundaries. It is less ideal for the current genomics-forward framing unless the plant-evolution material is explicitly tied to pharmacological interpretation.

*Plants* is a broad, open-access option whose stated scope includes medicinal plants, secondary metabolites, phytomedicine, plant biotechnology, evolutionary biology, and biological control. It is a reasonable fallback for the current interdisciplinary package, subject to the authors' publication-cost and selectivity preferences.

## Recommendation

Submit a presubmission query to *Phytochemistry Reviews* first. If the editor indicates that the antiparasitic and evolutionary-genomics integration is outside the journal's preferred balance, use *Journal of Ethnopharmacology* for a pharmacology-led revision or *Plants* for the broad interdisciplinary version. Do not target JXB without first obtaining a positive presubmission response.
